## Why, Though?

A natural question, should you have come across this site, is 'Why, though?' Some of you may not have any questions about it. For some, to have your personal and professional presence on the internet be wrapped in an emulation of a 1980's computer game needs no explanation. It's a choice, though, and one that I acknowledge has tradeoffs. Many of the incentives in place today ask you to genericize yourself, to make yourself paletteable to the greatest number of people. The issue with this, of course, is that no one is for everyone, and one of the most important things you can do to live a happy and fulfilling life is to surround yourself with people who you feel comfortable being yourself around. Being yourself keeps you from wasting time being someone you don't really want to be. 

So then why Rogue? The first computer I ever used was a 1980's Zenith running MS DOS. This was in the mid 90's. My parents had acquired it a some point, and since this was still pre home-0internet, and most things were still on paper, there wasn't a driving need to upgrade it. It was also an absolute tank. The screen wasn't even three color, the whole thing weighed something like thirty pounds, there was no mouse, and it took 5 1/2 inch floppy discs. There was Word Perfect, and probably some other things for real work, but most importantly it had _games_. 3D Battle Chess, Monopoly, Space Station: Oblivion, Star Trek, Alley Cat, Jeopardy, Space Invaders, and of course Rogue. Now given that I was 6, and not fluent in how the MS DOS command line interface worked, accessing the games seemed like divination. Over time, some games disappeared. Maybe they were deleted off of the floppy discs, or maybe we just forgot the incantations. Rogue was one of those games. It was there for a while, and I happily hacked and slashed my way through the dungeon, and then it was gone. 

It was an interesting paradox of the machine that it was both constant and ephemeral. I'm certain that it ran as good as new up to the day my parents sent it to e-waste recycling, and yet because I didn't understand it, it was a matter of chance as to what I could access on a given day. Part of me wants to turn on that old computer again, revisit it with a working knowledge of computer science, and reconcile memory to reality. Given the aforementioned recycling, that won’t happen. What I can do, and what I’m trying to do here, is revisit the wonder and joy, share it with others, and hopefully brighten your day just a little bit.
